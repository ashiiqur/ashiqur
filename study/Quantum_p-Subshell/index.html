Quantum p-Subshell

Key insight: This is the exact reason why Noble Gases (like Neon or Argon) are so incredibly stable and unreactive! Their outermost electrons are in completely filled $p$ subshells. Instead of having "pointy" orbitals sticking out in different directions looking to bond, they are surrounded by a perfectly smooth, spherical shield of negative charge.
⭐ Merge (Adding the Math)

In quantum mechanics, an atom doesn't view a full $p$ subshell as three separate, isolated boxes. It experiences one total, combined electron environment.

To find the shape of that total environment, we have to mathematically add the individual probability densities together: $\vert{}p_x\vert{}^2 + \vert{}p_y\vert{}^2 + \vert{}p_z\vert{}^2$.

When the slider is at zero, the simulation shows you the three orbitals as if they were separate, solid plastic objects (like three balloons tied together in the center).

As you increase the Merge slider, the simulation starts performing that mathematical addition, blending them from separate distinct shapes into a single, unified field.
